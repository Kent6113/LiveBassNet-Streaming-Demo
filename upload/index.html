LiveBassNet Studio

Load a song without its bass, press play, and steer the generated bass line by dragging the point on the pad. Everything runs in this browser tab: the audio is analysed and a strictly causal BassNet generates the bass here, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

What happens. The file is decoded, mixed to mono and resampled to 16 kHz. The page then computes the same causal constant-Q transform used in training (264 bins, A0 upwards, 10 ms hop, each frame ending at its own timestamp). The model is the recommended streaming BassNet (10/640 window, log-magnitude input, trained on Slakh2100). It runs with onnxruntime-web, on the GPU through WebGPU when the browser offers it, otherwise on several CPU threads. Each prediction uses only audio up to that frame. Notes are decided causally, one frame after the onset curve peaks, and played by a simple synth. The pitch goes through a causal smoothing filter first (a forward pass over the 88 semitones that favours staying on the current note; “Pitch smoothing” is that preference, 0 = off), which removes most of the rapid back-and-forth between notes and adds no delay.

Moving z. When you let go of the point (and every 0.3 s while dragging), the bass is recomputed from just ahead of the playhead onwards. The timeline shows how far the new z has got. On a fast GPU this keeps ahead of playback. On a CPU it can run slower than real time, and parts not yet recomputed play the previous z.

Limits. The model was trained on synthesised Slakh2100 songs, so real recordings are out of its training domain. A song that already contains bass mostly makes it double that bass. The pad shading is the average note rate over three Slakh test songs, to give a sense of direction only.
